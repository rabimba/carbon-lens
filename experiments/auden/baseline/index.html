Ann Arbor, Michigan · Autumn 1941 · Two Undergraduate Credits

Literature’s Most Notorious Seminar, Reborn Without the Sleepless Nights.

In September 1941, thirty-four-year-old poet W. H. Auden walked into Room 2225, Angell Hall wearing a faded jersey and worn sneakers, banned note-taking, and handed stunned undergraduates a single mimeographed sheet: 41 works spanning more than 6,000 required pages—from Aeschylus, Augustine, and Dante to Shakespeare, Mozart, Wagner, Dostoevsky, Melville, Muncie anthropology, and Kafka.

When Auden unveiled his final evaluation—including memorizing six cantos of Dante—a student delegation formally protested it as “impossible and inhumane.” Here, every grueling ordeal becomes an engaging scholarly studio grounded in Auden’s own critical writings.
